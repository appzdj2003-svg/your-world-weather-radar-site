Watch vs. Warning vs. Advisory: What Weather Alerts Mean

Home / Guides / Watch vs. warning

The National Weather Service (NWS) uses three main alert levels. Knowing the difference tells you how much time you have.

AlertMeaningWhat to do
WatchConditions are favorable for the hazard. It may happen, but it isn't happening yet.Be prepared. Review your plan and know where you'd shelter.
WarningThe hazard is happening or about to happen.Take action now to protect life and property.
AdvisoryA less serious hazard that can still cause significant inconvenience.Be aware and use caution.

An easy way to remember

NWS compares it to cooking: a watch means the ingredients for severe weather are in place; a warning means the recipe is happening now.

Who issues them

  • Tornado and severe thunderstorm watches usually come from NOAA's Storm Prediction Center and cover large areas for several hours.
  • Warnings come from your local NWS forecast office and are drawn as polygons around the threatened area.
  • Hurricane watches and warnings come from the National Hurricane Center.

Getting alerts

Use more than one way to get warnings: Wireless Emergency Alerts on your phone, NOAA Weather Radio, local media and an alert app. Make sure your phone's emergency alerts are switched on.
