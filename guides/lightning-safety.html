Lightning Safety: When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors

Home / Guides / Lightning safety

The National Weather Service's message is simple: When thunder roars, go indoors. If you can hear thunder, you're close enough to be struck. Lightning can strike as far as 10 miles from the storm.

Safe shelter

  • A substantial building with electricity or plumbing.
  • A hard-topped metal vehicle with the windows up.

Picnic shelters, tents, dugouts and open-sided structures do not protect you.

Wait 30 minutes

Stay inside until 30 minutes after the last thunder you hear.

If you're caught outside

There's no safe place outdoors during a thunderstorm. If you can't reach shelter, NWS advises you to reduce your risk:

  • Get off elevated areas such as hills, ridges and peaks.
  • Never lie flat on the ground, and never shelter under an isolated tree.
  • Stay away from water, and from objects that conduct electricity, such as wire fences and power lines.
  • If you're in a group, spread out.

Indoors

Avoid corded phones, plumbing (showers, baths, sinks) and electrical equipment you're touching, and stay away from windows and doors.

If someone is struck

Call 911. Lightning victims don't carry an electrical charge and are safe to touch, so start CPR if needed.
