Off-Grid Weather Safety: Staying Informed Without Cell Signal

Home / Guides / Off-grid emergency weather

Hiking, camping, boating and back-road driving can take you out of cell coverage, where Wireless Emergency Alerts and apps can't reach you. Plan for that before you go.

1. Check before you lose signal

Look at the forecast and any watches or warnings for every place on your route before you leave coverage. Tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.

2. Carry NOAA Weather Radio

NOAA Weather Radio All Hazards broadcasts NWS warnings, watches and forecasts 24 hours a day from more than 1,000 transmitters, on seven VHF frequencies between 162.400 and 162.550 MHz. It needs no cell signal. Many radios support SAME codes so they only sound for your county.

3. Know your phone's satellite features

Some newer phones can send emergency or text messages by satellite when there's no cell or Wi-Fi signal. Availability depends on the phone model, the carrier, the country and your plan. Check your phone's settings before the trip and try any demo mode it offers. Carrier message and data rates may apply.

4. Emergency first

In a life-threatening emergency, call 911 or use your phone's Emergency SOS. In the U.S., you can text 911 in many areas. Where it isn't supported you'll get a bounce-back message, so call if you can.

5. Pack the basics

Water, food, layers, a light, a first-aid kit, a power bank and a paper map. Ready.gov's kit list is a good starting point.
