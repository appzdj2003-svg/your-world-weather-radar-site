Severe Thunderstorm Safety: Wind, Hail and What 'Severe' Means

Home / Guides / Severe thunderstorm safety

Every thunderstorm produces lightning. For lightning rules, such as when to go indoors and how long to wait, see our lightning safety guide. This page covers what makes a storm severe.

What "severe" means

The National Weather Service calls a thunderstorm severe when it produces at least one of:

  • Hail 1 inch in diameter or larger
  • Wind gusts of 58 mph or stronger
  • A tornado

Damaging wind

Most thunderstorm wind damage comes from straight-line winds, not tornadoes. They can down trees and power lines. On radar, a bow echo is a classic sign.

During a Severe Thunderstorm Warning

  • Go indoors to a sturdy building and stay away from windows.
  • Bring in or secure loose outdoor items before the storm if you can.
  • Don't drive through flooded roads (Turn Around, Don't Drown).
  • If the warning also mentions a possible tornado, follow your tornado shelter plan.
