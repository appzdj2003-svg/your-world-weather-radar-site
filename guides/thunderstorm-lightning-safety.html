Thunderstorm and lightning safety basics

Guides / Lightning safety

Lightning can strike well away from the rain shaft of a storm. The National Weather Service (NWS) sums up its guidance in one line: When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors.

Know the rule

  • If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck. There is no safe place outdoors during a thunderstorm.
  • Get inside a substantial building with wiring and plumbing, or a hard-topped metal vehicle with the windows up.
  • Picnic shelters, tents, dugouts and open-sided structures do not protect you.
  • Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before going back outside.

Indoors

  • Avoid corded phones, plumbing (showers, washing dishes) and electrical equipment that plugs into the wall.
  • Stay away from windows and doors, and off porches.

If you are caught outside

  • Get off elevated areas such as hills and ridges.
  • Never shelter under an isolated tree, and stay away from water and metal fences.
  • Keep moving toward a safe building or vehicle. These steps reduce risk; they do not make the outdoors safe.

Source: NWS Lightning Safety.
