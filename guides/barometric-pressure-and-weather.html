Barometric Pressure and Weather: What Rising and Falling Pressure Means

Home / Guides / Barometric pressure

Barometric (air) pressure is the weight of the air above you. It's measured in inches of mercury (inHg) in U.S. reports and in hectopascals (hPa), also called millibars (mb), almost everywhere else.

Standard sea-level pressure: 29.92 inHg = 1013.25 hPa.

High and low pressure

  • High pressure: air sinks, which tends to bring drier, more settled weather.
  • Low pressure: air rises, cools and condenses, which tends to bring clouds and precipitation. Storms form around low-pressure centers.

Watch the trend

The change over a few hours tells you more than a single reading. Steadily falling pressure often means an approaching low and worsening weather; rising pressure often means clearing. A very rapid drop can signal a strong storm.

Station vs. sea-level pressure

Pressure falls as you go higher, so weather reports adjust readings to sea level so places at different elevations can be compared. A home barometer usually needs to be calibrated to your local reported sea-level pressure.
