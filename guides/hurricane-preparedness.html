Hurricane Preparedness: Categories, Watches and a Simple Checklist

Home / Guides / Hurricane preparedness

The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30; the eastern Pacific season starts May 15. The National Hurricane Center (NHC) issues forecasts, the forecast cone and watches and warnings.

Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale

CategorySustained winds
174–95 mph
296–110 mph
3 (major)111–129 mph
4 (major)130–156 mph
5 (major)157 mph or higher

The scale covers wind only. Storm surge, rainfall flooding and tornadoes can be deadly at any category.

Watch vs. warning timing

  • Hurricane watch: hurricane conditions are possible, issued 48 hours before tropical-storm-force winds are expected.
  • Hurricane warning: hurricane conditions are expected, issued 36 hours ahead of tropical-storm-force winds.

The cone is not the impact zone

The NHC cone shows the probable track of the storm's center. Hazards, including surge and heavy rain, often reach far outside the cone.

Storm surge

Storm surge, the abnormal rise of seawater pushed ashore by the storm, has historically been the greatest threat to life along the coast. Know whether you live in an evacuation zone, and leave when local officials tell you to.

Checklist

  • Know your evacuation zone and route.
  • Build a kit: water (one gallon per person per day for several days), food, medicines, flashlight, batteries, first aid, a battery or hand-crank radio, chargers.
  • Copy important documents and keep them waterproof.
  • Plan for pets.
  • Check insurance, including flood insurance, which often has a waiting period.
